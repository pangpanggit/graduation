Updating a heart failure patient's risk every hospital day — using the progress notes clinicians already write.

Background

Patients hospitalized with heart failure (HF) can deteriorate rapidly during their stay. However, most HF mortality models estimate risk only once, using structured data collected at admission. Such a static score cannot reflect changes in symptoms, medications, or procedures that occur after admission.

Objective

To predict the risk of in-hospital death within the next 48 hours, updated every hospital day, using daily clinical progress notes.

Methods

We used a single-center retrospective cohort of hospitalized HF patients. Each patient-day was one prediction point: only notes written up to that day were used, and the label was in-hospital death within the following 48 hours.

Progress notes record symptoms, clinical judgment, medication changes such as vasopressors and inotropes, and major procedures such as dialysis and mechanical ventilation. These notes were converted into daily representations and modeled over time. Performance was compared with a static model based on admission-time structured data, using patient-level splits, AUROC, and AUPRC.

Conclusion

This study treats progress notes as dynamic clinical information rather than supplementary text, moving risk prediction from a one-time estimate toward continuous, day-level monitoring.
